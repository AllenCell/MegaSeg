## Chapter 1: Classic Segmentation Ground Truth Verification

**Objective:** Verify that classic `aicssegmentation` workflows reproduce the ground truth (GT) masks used for MegaSeg training and evaluation, across major library version upgrades (scikit-image 0.20->0.26, numpy 1.24->2.4, scipy 1.10->1.17).

**Issue:** [#3](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/3) | **PR:** [#15](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/15) (merged)

### Methods

We conducted a 4-phase verification to assess GT reliability:

- **Phase 1** (24 images, 12 structures): Compared pre-existing classic workflow outputs against GT masks from `test_new.csv`.
- **Phase 2** (81 images, 10 structures): Re-ran workflows fresh on per-structure CSVs (3 images/split x 3 splits x 9 structures + TOMM20 added in Phase 3b).
- **Phase 3** (24 images, 12 structures): The critical determinism experiment --- compared freshly-generated workflow outputs against previously-stored outputs (not GT) to test reproducibility across library versions.
- **Phase 3b** (30 images, 11 structures): Fresh runs on test-split images, including TOMM20 after fixing its numpy 2.x compatibility issue.

Four classic workflow scripts required source-level patches to run under the upgraded library stack:

| Workflow | Root Cause | Fix |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| LMNB1 | `watershed` moved from `skimage.morphology` to `skimage.segmentation` | Import redirect |
| CETN2 | `peak_local_max` returns coordinates instead of boolean mask (skimage 0.26) | Adapter function |
| SLC25A17 | Same `peak_local_max` API change | Same adapter |
| TOMM20 | Module-level notebook code triggers `ndarray.newbyteorder()` (removed in numpy 2.0) | AST-based source stripping |

All patches are applied at import time; no original workflow files were modified.

### Results

**Phase 3 (Determinism):** 22 of 24 images produced bit-identical outputs (Dice = 1.000) between fresh and existing classic workflow runs. Only ST6GAL1 (2 images) showed minor floating-point deviation (Dice = 0.997). Classic workflows are deterministic across library versions.

**Phase 1 & 2 (Workflow vs. GT):** Per-structure Dice scores between workflow output and GT masks:

| Structure | N Images | Mean Dice | Range | Tier |
|-----------|:--------:|:---------:|-------|------|
| NPM1 | 11 | 0.998 | 0.977--1.000 | Reliable |
| TOMM20 | 3 | 1.000 | 0.9996--1.000 | Reliable |
| CTNNB1 | 11 | 0.999 | 0.999--1.000 | Reliable |
| RAB5A | 11 | 0.998 | 0.997--0.998 | Reliable |
| LAMP1 | 2 | 0.999 | 0.998--0.999 | Reliable |
| NUP153 | 11 | 0.995 | 0.987--0.998 | Reliable |
| SON | 11 | 0.995 | 0.994--0.996 | Reliable |
| MYH10 | 11 | 0.961 | 0.949--0.976 | Moderate |
| TUBA1B | 2 | 0.963 | 0.927--1.000 | Moderate |
| SEC61B | 11 | 0.925 | 0.758--0.997 | Unreliable |
| ST6GAL1 | 11 | 0.905 | 0.867--0.999 | Unreliable |
| ACTN1 | 11 | 0.889 | 0.666--0.999 | Unreliable |
| FBL | 2 | 0.641 | 0.609--0.674 | Unreliable |

### Key Findings

1. **Classic workflows are deterministic across library versions.** The observed discrepancies between workflow outputs and GT masks are structural, not caused by software version drift.
2. **GT masks were not generated by the classic workflow scripts.** Investigation of original data generation scripts revealed that GT masks originated from a separate AICS production pipeline (`struct_seg_path` from `allcells_beta.csv`), not from the classic workflow scripts themselves. For FBL, the GT used a different algorithm (`dot_slice_by_slice` with log_sigma=1, cutoff=0.01).
3. **7 of 14 structures have reliable GT** (Dice > 0.99), 2 have moderate discrepancy (~0.95), and 4 show significant discrepancy (0.64--0.93) due to the different GT generation pipelines.

## Chapter 3: Backbone Architecture Comparison

**Objective:** Determine the best neural network backbone for MegaSeg's multi-structure segmentation by training and evaluating four architectures on all 14 structures.

**Issue:** [#5](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/5) | **PRs:** [#1](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/1), [#8](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/8) (merged)

### Methods

Four backbone architectures were trained as MegaSeg multi-structure models using cyto-dl with identical training data (14 structures), data augmentation, and training schedule. All models were evaluated on 67 test images per structure (938 total) with uint8-quantized predictions.

- **DynUNet** --- Dynamic convolutional U-Net with residual blocks
- **SwinUNETR** --- Swin Transformer-based encoder with U-Net decoder
- **AttentionUNet** --- Standard U-Net with attention gates
- **UNet** --- Standard convolutional U-Net

### Results

|----------|:---------:|:----:|
| **DynUNet** | **0.868** | 1 |
| SwinUNETR | 0.860 | 2 |

| UNet | 0.833 | 4 |

| GJA1 | 0.967 | 0.958 | 0.956 | 0.940 |

### Key Findings

1. **DynUNet is the best backbone** for multi-structure 3D segmentation, outperforming the Transformer-based SwinUNETR by 0.8 Dice points and the standard UNet by 3.5 points.
2. **All architectures struggle with NPM1** (nucleophosmin): Dice ranges from 0.572 (SwinUNETR) to 0.686 (AttentionUNet). This structure has a morphologically distinct punctate nuclear pattern that the multi-structure model finds difficult.
3. **DynUNet leads on 10 of 14 structures.** SwinUNETR outperforms on ATP2A2 and SEC61B; AttentionUNet on NPM1.
4. **The Transformer-based SwinUNETR does not outperform convolution-based architectures** on this task, likely because 3D subcellular fluorescence microscopy data benefits more from strong local feature extraction than long-range attention.

## Chapter 4: MegaSeg vs. Individual Per-Structure Models

**Objective:** Demonstrate that a single multi-structure MegaSeg model outperforms individually-trained per-structure specialist models.

**Issue:** [#6](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/6) | **PRs:** [#8](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/8), [#11](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/11), [#12](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/12) (all merged)

### Methods

14 individual DynUNet models were trained (one per structure) and compared against the single MegaSeg DynUNet model on the same test data (67 images/structure, 938 total). Additionally, the full 14x14 DL cross-evaluation matrix was computed: each individual model was applied to all 14 structures' test sets, producing 14 x 14 x 67 = 13,132 predictions.

**DL model 14x14 cross-evaluation** (Individual models applied cross-structure, diagonal = matched, off-diagonal = cross-applied):

The cross-eval overall mean Dice is **0.506**, confirming that individual models, like classic workflows, are specialized and fail when applied to non-target structures. Notably, some individual models show broader capability: e.g., TJP1's model achieves 0.800+ on DSP and 0.730+ on SON, while NPM1's model reaches 0.802 on CTNNB1.

### Key Findings

1. **MegaSeg outperforms individual models on 13 of 14 structures**, with a mean improvement of +0.101 Dice. The largest gains are on structures where individual models underperform most (GJA1: +0.245, RAB5A: +0.262).
2. **NPM1 is the only exception:** MegaSeg (0.610) underperforms the individual NPM1 model (0.805) by -0.195 Dice. This suggests multi-structure training may interfere with learning NPM1's unique nuclear punctate morphology.
3. **Multi-structure training provides a strong regularization effect.** The single MegaSeg model trained on all structures simultaneously learns shared representations that improve generalization, even outperforming models specifically optimized for each target.
4. **Individual model cross-eval (0.506 mean) vs. MegaSeg (0.868 mean)** demonstrates the fundamental advantage of the multi-structure approach.

## Chapter 5: Comparison Against SOTA Segmentation Methods

**Objective:** Benchmark MegaSeg against 7 alternative segmentation methods spanning trained models, zero-shot tools, and simple baselines to establish its position in the segmentation landscape.

**Issue:** [#20](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/20) | **PR:** [#31](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/31)

### Methods

Eight methods were evaluated on the same 14 structures x 67 test images (938 total):

| Method | Type | Training |
|--------|------|----------|
| **MegaSeg (DynUNet)** | Semantic | Trained (multi-structure, 14 structures) |
| **nnU-Net v2** | Semantic | Trained (3d_fullres, multi-class, fold 0) |
| **Individual DynUNet** | Semantic | Trained (per-structure, 14 models) |
| **Classic (aicsseg)** | Semantic | Rule-based workflows (per-structure) |

### Key Findings

1. **Classic workflows achieve the highest overall Dice (0.976)** because each workflow was hand-tuned for its target structure and the GT was derived from the same production pipeline. However, classic workflows cannot generalize: each requires a dedicated workflow per structure.
2. **MegaSeg (0.868) significantly outperforms nnU-Net v2 (0.829)** by 3.9 Dice points (p = 8.3e-74), despite nnU-Net being the gold-standard self-configuring segmentation framework. MegaSeg outperforms on 13/14 structures.
3. **nnU-Net outperforms MegaSeg only on NPM1** (0.923 vs 0.610), the same problematic structure for MegaSeg. This confirms the NPM1 difficulty is specific to MegaSeg's architecture/training, not an intrinsic data issue.
4. **Zero-shot methods (CellPose, StarDist, SAM) fail catastrophically** on subcellular fluorescence microscopy. All face a fundamental domain mismatch: pretrained on natural images (SAM), whole cells (CellPose), or nuclei (StarDist) --- none suited for subcellular organelle semantic segmentation. SAM achieves only 0.025 Dice, running 2D slice-by-slice with no cross-Z consistency.
5. **Otsu (0.376) establishes the baseline floor.** It performs reasonably on high-contrast structures (NPM1: 0.898, TJP1: 0.783) but fails on diffuse targets (RAB5A: 0.009).

## Chapter 6: LMNB1 Fine-Tuning and Pretraining Benefit

**Objective:** Quantify the benefit of MegaSeg pretraining when fine-tuning on a new, unseen structure (LMNB1), and compare against training from scratch under varying data availability and training duration.

**Issue:** [#6](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/6) | **PRs:** [#9](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/9), [#17](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/17), [#18](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/18) (all merged)

### Methods

A full 4x4 experiment grid was conducted for both fine-tuning (from MegaSeg-13 pretrained weights) and training from scratch:
- **Data fractions:** 100%, 50%, 20%, 10% of LMNB1 training data
- **Training checkpoints:** 10, 50, 100 epochs, and converged (best validation loss)

All experiments used the DynUNet backbone and were evaluated on 67 LMNB1 test images. The baseline (MegaSeg-13 applied to LMNB1 without any fine-tuning) achieves Dice = 0.297.

### Results

**Fine-tuned from MegaSeg-13 (mean Dice):**

| Data | 10 epochs | 50 epochs | 100 epochs | Converged |
|------|:---------:|:---------:|:----------:|:---------:|
| 100% | 0.908 | 0.945 | 0.949 | **0.958** |
| 50% | 0.879 | 0.932 | 0.945 | 0.952 |
| 20% | 0.703 | 0.900 | 0.921 | 0.945 |
| 10% | 0.699 | 0.895 | 0.914 | 0.942 |

**Trained from scratch (mean Dice):**

| Data | 10 epochs | 50 epochs | 100 epochs | Converged |
|------|:---------:|:---------:|:----------:|:---------:|
| 100% | 0.535 | 0.839 | 0.895 | 0.935 |
| 50% | 0.264 | 0.787 | 0.854 | 0.902 |
| 20% | 0.097 | 0.474 | 0.699 | 0.832 |
| 10% | 0.103 | 0.469 | 0.694 | 0.805 |

**Pretraining benefit (Fine-tune minus Scratch, Delta Dice):**

| Data | 10 epochs | 50 epochs | 100 epochs | Converged |
|------|:---------:|:---------:|:----------:|:---------:|
| 100% | **+0.373** | +0.105 | +0.054 | +0.022 |
| 50% | **+0.616** | +0.145 | +0.090 | +0.050 |
| 20% | **+0.606** | +0.426 | +0.222 | +0.113 |
| 10% | **+0.596** | +0.425 | +0.220 | **+0.137** |

### Key Findings

1. **MegaSeg pretraining provides the largest benefit in low-data, low-epoch regimes.** At 10% data and 10 epochs, fine-tuning achieves 0.699 Dice vs. 0.103 from scratch --- a +0.596 advantage. This is the most practically relevant scenario: rapid adaptation to a new structure with minimal annotation.
2. **The benefit decreases as more data and training time are available,** but never vanishes: even at 100% data and full convergence, pretraining provides +0.022 Dice (0.958 vs. 0.935).
3. **Fine-tuning with only 10% of the data converges to 0.942 Dice** --- nearly matching the 100% data result (0.958). From scratch with 10% data converges to only 0.805.
4. **The baseline (no fine-tuning) is 0.297**, confirming that MegaSeg-13 cannot segment LMNB1 without adaptation, but its learned representations transfer effectively once fine-tuned.

## Chapter 7: Drug Perturbation Evaluation

**Objective:** Evaluate MegaSeg's robustness when segmenting drug-perturbed cells, where morphological changes may differ substantially from the training distribution.

**Issue:** [#7](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/7) | **PRs:** [#19](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/19) (merged), [#35](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/35)

### Methods

Two evaluation rounds were conducted on drug-perturbed fluorescence microscopy data. **No ground truth segmentation exists for these FOVs**, so evaluation is qualitative (visual comparison of segmentation overlays).

**v1 (S3 multi-channel, 108 FOVs):**
- 6 structures: ACTB, NUP153, ST6GAL1, TJP1, TOMM20, TUBA1B
- 10 drug conditions: Jasplakinolide, Latrunculin A, Thapsigargin, Brefeldin A, Monensin, Staurosporine, Rotenone, Nocodazole, Paclitaxel (+ controls)
- 3 model types compared: MegaSeg (DynUNet), Individual per-structure models, Classic workflows

**v2 (local single-channel TIFFs, 48 FOVs):**
- 5 structures: FBL, NUP153, ST6GAL1, TOMM20, TUBA1B
- Drug conditions: Actinomycin, Thapsigargin, Rotenone (high/low dose), Nocodazole (+ controls)
- 3 model types compared with cross-structure proxies where no structure-specific model exists (FBL uses SON checkpoint, TUBA1B uses TJP1 checkpoint)

| Model | Coverage (v1) | Coverage (v2) |
|-------|:-----:|:-----:|
| MegaSeg | 6/6 structures | 5/5 structures |
| Individual | 4/6 | 5/5 (with proxies) |
| Classic | 5/6 | 5/5 |

### Results

This is a **qualitative comparison**. Key observations from visual inspection of segmentation overlays:

1. **MegaSeg maintains coherent segmentation under drug perturbation** across all tested structures and conditions. The model produces spatially consistent 3D predictions even when cellular morphology is substantially altered.
2. **Classic workflows are sensitive to intensity distribution shifts** caused by drug treatment, particularly for structures where the workflow relies on global intensity thresholds.
3. **Individual models show intermediate robustness** --- better than classic workflows on perturbed data but less consistent than MegaSeg across diverse perturbation types.

For reference, standard (non-perturbed) test set Dice on the overlapping structures:

| Structure | Individual | MegaSeg | Delta |
|-----------|:---------:|:-------:|:-----:|
| NUP153 | 0.787 | 0.942 | +0.155 |
| ST6GAL1 | 0.791 | 0.878 | +0.087 |
| TJP1 | 0.784 | 0.932 | +0.148 |
| TOMM20 | 0.894 | 0.915 | +0.021 |

### Key Findings

1. **MegaSeg generalizes to drug-perturbed data** that is out-of-distribution relative to its training set, suggesting that multi-structure training produces more robust feature representations.
2. **No quantitative evaluation is possible** without ground truth segmentation masks for drug-perturbed conditions. Future work should consider generating reference annotations for a subset of perturbed FOVs to enable quantitative comparison.
3. **The comparison provides practical evidence** that MegaSeg can be used as a general-purpose segmentation tool in drug screening and perturbation biology contexts.

## Chapter 8: Ablation --- Training Set Diversity

**Objective:** Determine how the number and diversity of structures in the training set affects MegaSeg's segmentation performance, generalization to held-out structures, and internal network health.

**Issue:** [#22](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/22) | **PR:** [#24](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/24)

### Methods

Five models were trained with increasing numbers of structures, all using the DynUNet backbone:

| Model | # Structures | Training Structures |
|-------|:------------:|---------------------|
| 2-struct | 2 | TOMM20, TJP1 |
| 4-struct | 4 | ACTN1, ATP2A2, CTNNB1, MYH10 |
| 7-struct | 7 | ACTN1, ATP2A2, CTNNB1, MYH10, RAB5A, ST6GAL1, TOMM20 |
| 14-struct | 14 | All 14 structures |
| MegaSeg-13 | 13 | All except AAVS1 (pretrained, production model) |

All models were evaluated on the same filtered test set: **57 samples per structure** (798 total), after excluding the bottom 10 samples per structure by Dice score to remove low-signal artifacts. Bottleneck health was assessed by counting dead channels (max activation = 0 across all test samples) at each layer.

### Results

**Overall performance:**

| Model | Overall Dice | Seen Dice | Held-out Dice | Bottleneck Dead % |
|-------|:-----------:|:---------:|:------------:|:-----------------:|
| 2-struct | 0.651 | 0.909 | 0.608 | **0%** |
| 4-struct | 0.619 | 0.865 | 0.521 | 9.1% |
| 7-struct | 0.714 | 0.888 | 0.540 | **100%** |
| 14-struct | **0.897** | **0.897** | --- | **100%** |
| MegaSeg-13 | 0.864 | 0.908 | 0.285 | **100%** |

**Per-structure Dice (selected structures showing key patterns):**

| Structure | 2-struct | 4-struct | 7-struct | 14-struct | MegaSeg-13 |
|-----------|:--------:|:--------:|:--------:|:---------:|:----------:|
| TOMM20 (seen in all) | **0.924** | 0.765 | **0.926** | 0.915 | 0.934 |
| TJP1 (seen in 2-str) | **0.894** | 0.661 | 0.655 | **0.931** | **0.940** |
| CTNNB1 (seen 4+) | 0.776 | **0.960** | **0.964** | 0.955 | 0.965 |
| GJA1 (seen 14 only) | 0.638 | 0.351 | 0.389 | **0.967** | **0.972** |
| DSP (seen 14 only) | 0.506 | 0.249 | 0.515 | **0.923** | **0.921** |
| AAVS1 (unseen in all except 14) | 0.313 | 0.220 | 0.216 | **0.887** | 0.285 |

**Bottleneck health by layer:**

| Model | Bottleneck (320ch) | downsamples.2 (256ch) | upsamples.0 (256ch) |
|-------|:------------------:|:---------------------:|:-------------------:|
| 2-struct | 0/320 (0%) | 0/256 (0%) | 0/256 (0%) |
| 4-struct | 29/320 (9.1%) | 0/256 (0%) | 0/256 (0%) |
| 7-struct | 320/320 (100%) | 0/256 (0%) | 0/256 (0%) |
| 14-struct | 320/320 (100%) | 0/256 (0%) | 172/256 (67.2%) |
| MegaSeg-13 | 320/320 (100%) | 26/256 (10.2%) | 170/256 (66.4%) |

### Key Findings

1. **Training diversity monotonically improves overall performance:** Dice rises from 0.651 (2-struct) to 0.897 (14-struct). Including more structures forces the network to learn more generalizable representations.
2. **The gap between seen and held-out performance is dramatic for smaller models:** 2-struct achieves 0.909 on seen structures but only 0.608 on held-out; MegaSeg-13 achieves 0.908 on seen but only 0.285 on its single held-out structure (AAVS1).
3. **Complete bottleneck collapse occurs at 7+ training structures** (100% of 320 channels dead), yet segmentation performance continues to improve. This indicates the network bypasses the bottleneck entirely via skip connections, rendering the deepest encoder layers effectively non-functional.
4. **The bottleneck collapse worsens with depth in larger models:** at 14 structures, 67% of upsamples.0 channels are also dead, suggesting the collapse propagates from the bottleneck outward.
5. **The 2-struct model is the only one with a fully healthy bottleneck** (0% dead channels). Collapse begins between 4 and 7 training structures.

## Chapter 9: Feature Analysis and Interpretability

**Objective:** Analyze MegaSeg's internal feature representations across encoder and decoder layers to understand how the network organizes structural information and to characterize the bottleneck collapse phenomenon.

**Issue:** [#23](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/issues/23) | **PRs:** [#29](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/29), [#33](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/33), [#34](https://github.com/AllenCell/MegaSeg/pull/34)

### Methods

Global average-pooled (GAP) features were extracted from 6 layers of the MegaSeg-13 DynUNet model for 16 structures (14 standard + AAVS1, LMNB1), 10 test samples per structure. Four analysis methods were applied:

| Analysis | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)** | Pairwise representational similarity between structures |
| **Cosine similarity** | Geometric alignment of feature vectors |
| **Probing classifiers** | Structure classification accuracy from features (linear, 5-fold CV) |
| **UMAP / t-SNE / PCA** | 2D projections for visual cluster assessment |

Layers analyzed (with feature dimensionality):

| Layer | Dimensions | Position |
|-------|:----------:|----------|
| input_block | 32 | Shallowest encoder |
| downsamples.0 | 64 | Mid-shallow |
| downsamples.1 | 128 | Mid-encoder |
| downsamples.2 | 256 | Deepest encoder |
| upsamples.2 | 64 | Shallow decoder |
| upsamples.1 | 128 | Mid decoder |

### Results

**Probing classifier accuracy by layer (16-class, chance = 6.25%):**

| Layer | Accuracy | Std | vs. Chance |
|-------|:--------:|:---:|:----------:|
| input_block (32d) | 78.1% | 7.1% | +71.9% |
| downsamples.0 (64d) | 88.1% | 3.6% | +81.9% |
| **downsamples.1 (128d)** | **93.8%** | **4.4%** | **+87.5%** |
| downsamples.2 (256d) | **71.2%** | 6.1% | +64.9% |
| upsamples.2 (64d) | 91.2% | 8.5% | +84.9% |
| upsamples.1 (128d) | 90.6% | 5.2% | +84.4% |

**CKA similarity patterns:**

| Layer | Mean CKA | Trend |
|-------|:--------:|-------|
| input_block | 0.147 | Low --- structures are distinct |
| downsamples.0 | 0.208 | Increasing |
| downsamples.1 | 0.268 | Increasing |
| downsamples.2 | 0.309 | High --- structures converging |
| upsamples.2 | 0.306 | Maintained |
| upsamples.1 | 0.408 | Highest --- structures most similar |

Most similar structure pairs at downsamples.1: NUP153-ST6GAL1 (CKA = 0.730), AAVS1-SON (0.620).
Most dissimilar: DSP-TJP1 (0.028), DSP-ST6GAL1 (0.043).

**Cosine similarity at downsamples.2 (bottleneck):** All pairwise values > 0.98 (range 0.982--0.998), confirming near-identical representations --- consistent with feature collapse.

**Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) across layers:**

| Layer Pair | RSA Correlation |
|------------|:---------------:|
| input_block -- downsamples.0 | 0.689 |
| downsamples.0 -- downsamples.1 | 0.838 |
| downsamples.1 -- downsamples.2 | 0.308 |
| input_block -- downsamples.2 | **0.075** |
| downsamples.0 -- downsamples.2 | 0.142 |

**Per-structure F1 at the collapsed layer (downsamples.2) --- worst performers:**

| Structure | F1 (downsamples.2) | F1 (downsamples.1) | Drop |
|-----------|:-------------------:|:-------------------:|:----:|
| TJP1 | 0.313 | 0.800 | -0.487 |
| MYH10 | 0.447 | 0.893 | -0.446 |
| ACTN1 | 0.593 | 0.893 | -0.300 |
| LMNB1 | 0.593 | 1.000 | -0.407 |

### Key Findings

1. **Bottleneck collapse is unambiguously confirmed.** Probing accuracy drops from 93.8% at downsamples.1 to 71.2% at downsamples.2 --- a 22.6-point drop. Cosine similarity at downsamples.2 is > 0.98 for all structure pairs, meaning all structures have nearly identical representations at the deepest encoder layer.
2. **downsamples.1 (128-dim) is the best layer for structure discrimination** with 93.8% accuracy across 16 structures. This layer preserves rich, structure-specific information while being deep enough to capture complex morphological features.
3. **The decoder recovers discriminative information from skip connections, not from the bottleneck.** upsamples.2 (91.2%) and upsamples.1 (90.6%) nearly match the pre-collapse accuracy, demonstrating that skip connections effectively bypass the dead bottleneck.
4. **RSA confirms a representational discontinuity at the bottleneck.** downsamples.2 is nearly uncorrelated with input_block (r = 0.075) and downsamples.0 (r = 0.142), but the adjacent downsamples.0-downsamples.1 correlation is 0.838. The bottleneck develops a fundamentally different (and uninformative) representational structure.
5. **CKA increases with depth (0.147 to 0.408),** indicating progressive convergence of representations across structures. The network's deeper layers emphasize shared low-level features over structure-specific information.
6. **DSP (desmoplakin) is the most representationally distinct structure** across all layers, with consistently the lowest CKA to other structures. This aligns with its unique cell junction morphology.
